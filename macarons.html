Macaron shell ingredients

  • 100g egg whites, at room temperature
  • 100g granulated sugar
  • 140g almond flour, super-fine
  • 120g powdered sugar
  • A pinch of salt
  • Gel food coloring (optional—do not use liquid)
  • Easy buttercream filling
  • 170g unsalted butter, softened
  • 187g powdered sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 60g heavy cream, at room temperature

  • Instructions

    Wipe down your mixing bowl and whisk attachment with white vinegar or lemon juice to eliminate any trace of grease, which can prevent the egg whites from whipping properly.
  • Line two baking sheets with parchment paper or silicone mats. Use a macaron template under the paper to help you pipe uniform shells.
  • Combine the almond flour, powdered sugar, and salt in a food processor and pulse for about 5 seconds to achieve a very fine texture. Sift the mixture into a bowl and discard any large almond pieces.
  • Using a stand or hand mixer, beat room-temperature egg whites on medium speed until they become frothy.
  • Gradually add the granulated sugar while continuing to beat on medium-high speed. Once all the sugar is added, whip until the meringue is thick, glossy, and holds stiff peaks. You should be able to hold the whisk upside down without the peaks collapsing.
  • If using food coloring, add a few drops of gel color and mix on low speed until combined.
  • Add about half of your sifted dry ingredients to the meringue.
  • Using a rubber spatula, gently fold the batter using a "J" motion, sweeping around the edge and cutting through the center. Add the remaining dry ingredients and continue folding.
  • Check the batter's consistency frequently. You'll know it's ready when you can lift the spatula and the batter falls off in a thick, slow ribbon that you can form into a figure eight before it disappears back into itself.
  • Transfer the batter to a piping bag fitted with a round tip. Pipe 1.5-inch circles onto your prepared baking sheets, holding the bag perpendicular to the tray
  • . Once all the shells are piped, firmly tap the baking sheets on the counter several times to release trapped air bubbles. Pop any remaining bubbles with a toothpick.
  • Let the macarons rest at room temperature for 30–60 minutes, or until the surface is dry to the touch.
  • While resting, preheat your oven to 300°F (150°C).
  • Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for 18–22 minutes. The shells are done when they have "feet" (lol) and don't wobble when gently touched.
  • Allow the shells to cool completely on the baking sheet before peeling them off. For the filling: In a bowl, beat the softened butter until creamy. Sift in the powdered sugar and mix until combined. Add the vanilla and heavy cream, then whip until light and fluffy.
  • Transfer the buttercream to a piping bag. Pair up cooled macaron shells of similar sizes. Pipe a dollop of filling onto one shell and gently press a second shell on top.
  • If they are not room temperature, bring the macarons to room temperature for a few minutes before serving. Byeee XD
